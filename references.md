# Selected Related Work

These papers provide context, not a systematic review or proof of novelty. Bibliographic details and abstract-level descriptions were checked against the linked primary records when preparing this note. The repository does not claim to survey all work available in September 2026.

1. **Sharma, M., et al. (2023; revised 2025).** *Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models.* [arXiv:2310.13548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548).

   Relevant to the distinction between agreeing with users and supporting accurate judgment. This paper does not establish the proposed excessive-deferral failure or the effectiveness of a trajectory intervention.

2. **Kadavath, S., et al. (2022).** *Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know.* [arXiv:2207.05221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221).

   Relevant to model self-evaluation and the need to validate confidence estimates. A calibrated probability and a cost-sensitive stopping policy remain different objects of evaluation.

3. **Buçinca, Z., Malaya, M. B., and Gajos, K. Z. (2021).** *To Trust or to Think: Cognitive Forcing Functions Can Reduce Overreliance on AI in AI-assisted Decision-making.* [arXiv:2102.09692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09692). [DOI:10.1145/3449287](https://doi.org/10.1145/3449287).

   Relevant to evaluating decision quality together with user burden and subjective experience. Its results should not be generalized directly to long-term conversational assistants.

4. **Laban, P., Hayashi, H., Zhou, Y., and Neville, J. (2025).** *LLMs Get Lost In Multi-Turn Conversation.* [arXiv:2505.06120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06120).

   Directly adjacent work on performance and recovery in multi-turn interaction. It is a reason to avoid presenting multi-turn failure as a new discovery. This note proposes making investigation cost and justified closure explicit in a narrower troubleshooting setting.

## Positioning still to investigate

A fuller review should cover sequential decision-making and value of information, active diagnosis, selective prediction, multi-turn agent evaluation, belief revision, human reliance on AI, and interactive information seeking. The existing literature may already contain a suitable evaluation framework. If so, adapting that framework would be preferable to inventing new terminology or a redundant benchmark.
